## Prompting Techniques (Videos 1–13)

**1. Chain of Thought (CoT) Prompting** — guides the LLM to reason step-by-step rather than only giving final answers; examples in math, logic, and scheduling. Breaking problems into intermediate steps improves accuracy, clarity, and transparency.

**2. Few-Shot Prompting** — provide examples *within* the prompt to guide the response format. Sample inputs teach the AI a specific style (translations, math problems, interview-question generation) for precise, structured outputs.

**3. Role-Play Prompting** — assign a specific persona to the AI to shape tailored, realistic responses. Different roles change tone and advice; ideal for simulating experts and personalizing interactions.

**4. Iterative Prompting** — refine outputs through follow-up questions rather than a single prompt; gradually improve clarity, tone, and audience fit with layered instructions. Best for complex, evolving tasks (co-creation).

**5. Zero-Shot Prompting** — rely on the model's training with no examples for flexible responses; contrasted with few-shot. Choosing the right strategy among the prompt types greatly improves response quality.

**6. Using Parameters to Control Output** — control responses via parameters: tone, style, persona, length, and creativity. Tuning these tailors outputs to different audiences and tasks (business, education, creative work).

**7. Common Prompting Mistakes** — five to avoid: vagueness, multi-task overload, missing context, ignoring output formatting, and not iterating. Clear, specific, contextual prompts refined through iteration yield better responses.

**8. Prompt Engineering** — a vital professional skill: crafting clear, contextual, concise inputs. The **"3 C's" — Clarity, Context, Conciseness.** A strategic communication skill for productivity and problem-solving.

**9. Inside ChatGPT — How It Interprets Your Prompts** — ChatGPT processes prompts via tokenization, context awareness, and probability modeling. Four key elements of a strong prompt: **instruction, context, format, and constraints.** Understanding how it "thinks" enables clearer communication.

**10. Prompt Library** — a collection of reusable prompts that boosts productivity and consistency; organizing and refining prompts by task/domain lets you scale AI use across professional scenarios.

**11. OpenAI Playground** — a no-code web tool to experiment with models: test prompts, adjust parameters (temperature, token limits), and study AI behavior to improve prompting skills.

**12. Types of Prompts (Part A)** — **Instructional** prompts (direct tasks with clear goals/constraints — emails, summaries) and **Exploratory** prompts (spark creativity and idea generation — brainstorming, strategy).

**13. Types of Prompts (Part B)** — **Conversational** (ongoing dialogue), **Structured** (organized outputs like tables/lists), and **Clarifying** (refining tone or content). Together with instructional and exploratory, these five form the core prompting toolkit.
