# Module 9: Leadership with Generative AI

> Extracted from ISB / Emeritus course 17878 for personal study. Faculty: Prof. Vishal. Added June 2026.

## Overview & Learning Outcomes

Prof. Vishal covers the transformative impact of generative AI on leadership — effects on jobs, future trends, ownership challenges, and strategic adaptation. Frameworks include the **jagged frontier**, techniques such as **RAG** and chain-of-thought reasoning, and practical strategies to balance productivity gains with ethical concerns. By the end you can: identify and navigate AI's jagged frontier via active experimentation; understand AI's future in techniques like RAG and the shifting role of data; and manage ownership ambiguities with proactive IP policies.

**Key activities (due Wednesday, June 10, 2026):** Videos 1–6. Self-study: Assignment 9.1 (The AI Automation Experiment); Case Study: Generative AI and the Future of Work (HBS).

## Case Study: Generative AI and the Future of Work

A Harvard Business School case investigating the impacts of generative AI on workplace productivity, value creation, and labour markets — strategic implications, potential disruption to job roles, and opportunities for organisational adaptation.

## Video Lectures

**1. Leading with Generative AI** — references the future-of-work case; notes ChatGPT's 2023 launch reaching 100M users in two months; the tension between AI's benefits (automation, creativity) and threats to high-skill jobs; sets the goal of building leadership frameworks.

**2. Navigating AI's Impact on Jobs and Adoption** — a four-part leadership canvas (job/market effects, future paths, ownership redefinition, leader strategies). Explains technology adoption via **S-curve** models (Tesla example, innovators → early majority); generative AI's fast diffusion is slowed by risks like privacy and workflow reengineering.

**3. AI's Future Trends** — progress beyond bigger models: autonomous task execution (e.g., radiology), enhanced generalisation via zero/few-shot learning, chain-of-thought reasoning for error reduction; open-source vs. proprietary models; **RAG** for real-time data; multimodal capabilities (text/image/audio/video); a shift toward human-AI collaboration needing ethics training.

**4. AI, Ownership, and Innovation Challenges** — questions AI-output ownership amid blurred IP lines; references the Writers Guild strike and fair-use debates over training data; human vs. AI learning analogies; attribution in RAG; plagiarism in co-created works; credit for employee AI use.

**5. AI Leadership: Key Takeaways** — parallels to computer-era job creation (McKinsey's ~15M jobs estimate); urges nine leadership steps: transparent labour communication, centralised skilling, new ethics roles, adaptable IP policies, regulatory preparation, flexible structures, well-being support, employee input on policy, and industry partnerships.

**6. Reflecting on AI Leadership** — reflective questions on being an AI accelerator vs. skeptic (business and societal views); leadership in ambiguity for stakeholders; takeaways on experimenting at AI's frontier, technique-driven futures, ownership management, and workforce strategies.

## Self-Study Assignment 9.1: The AI Automation Experiment

Act as a **"Productivity Architect"** for your own job: use generative AI to automate as much of your day-to-day work as possible.

1. **Select tools** — any GenAI tools you can access (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot, etc.).
2. **Identify tasks** — a diverse set from your workload (drafting emails, analysing data, brainstorming, creating visuals).
3. **Run trials** — push the tools through complex workflows, not just simple questions.
4. **Observe** — where AI succeeds, hallucinates, saves time, or adds friction.

**Reflection (max 500 words)** covering: **The Experiment** (which tasks, your prompting approach, tangible results — time saved? quality?); **The Spectrum of Impression** (impressed or unimpressed — be honest); **The Forecast** (will the technology primarily REPLACE your tasks or AUGMENT your capabilities?). This "ground truth" anchors the module's discussion on how leaders integrate these technologies.
