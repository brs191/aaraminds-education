Introduction

AT&T Business Technology Product Development runs the Sales Tooling ecosystem — Salesforce, ROME, ADOPT, SSP, and Business Center — that powers the bank of business-critical sell, market, order, and manage flows for AT&T’s products. This AI-enabled business model reimagines those functions through human–AI collaboration, not automation alone, so the organisation can deliver more engineering and business outcomes at a lower cost base (the ~15% budget-reduction mandate), with faster go-to-market and higher quality. It applies the Level 3 organisational model (human augments machine, machine augments human, and reimagined business processes), a governed operating model, and a phased adoption path.

2. Operating Model (how it runs)

Data pipeline: consolidate telemetry, tickets, design documents, and Sales Tooling data with governance and access control. Algorithms: ML and LLM models for prediction, retrieval, and generation using AT&T-enabled tools (GitHub Copilot, Claude, GPT). Software infrastructure: Azure-first, multi-vendor, integrated with existing legacy systems. Experimentation platform: a governed proof-of-concept and evaluation harness (building on the Model Context Protocol server PoC and preferred coding-practice benchmarks) validates each model before production, with human-in-the-loop review for customer- and network-impacting outputs.

3. Value Creation and KPIs

Expected value: reduced development cycle time and go-to-market; fewer production incidents and faster resolution; reusable engineering knowledge across teams; lower cloud cost; and stronger sales enablement. Measured through cycle-time reduction, defect/incident rates, mean-time-to-resolution, cloud-cost savings, and adoption. [YOUR EXPERIENCE: insert defensible target figures where you have them.]

4. Barriers and Mitigations (REAL approach)

Principal barriers are data quality and access, governance and security, engineering-team adoption, and skill gaps. These are mitigated through AI SPOCs for knowledge sharing, reference coding scaffolds for consistency, AI awareness and demo sessions to build pull, and a phased AI use-case roadmap (the team’s 13 identified use cases sequenced across three quarters) so change is delivered in governed, low-risk increments.
