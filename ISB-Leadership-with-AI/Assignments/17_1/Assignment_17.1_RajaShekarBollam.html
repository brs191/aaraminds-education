1. Context

AT&T’s Sales Tooling ecosystem (Salesforce, ROME, ADOPT, SSP, Business Center) and the AI use cases we are introducing across the software development lifecycle and production support operate on sensitive customer, pricing, credit, and transaction data, and increasingly inform customer-facing sales and ordering decisions. That makes Responsible AI a precondition, not an afterthought. This document selects the three Responsible AI principles (from the NITI Aayog Responsible AI for All framework) most relevant to AT&T and sets out an approach to operationalise them using the four functions of the NIST AI Risk Management Framework — Govern, Map, Measure, Manage.

2. Top Three Responsible AI Principles for AT&T (and why)

PrincipleWhy it is most relevant to AT&T
Privacy & SecurityOur AI touches customer PII/CPNI, credit-check, billing, and transaction data. A privacy or security failure is both a regulatory and a trust risk at telecom scale.
Transparency & ExplainabilityAI-assisted outputs feed sales, pricing, credit, and production-support decisions. Engineers, sellers, auditors, and customers must be able to understand why an output was produced.
AccountabilityBusiness-critical, customer-impacting decisions require clear human ownership, review, and the ability to trace and contest an AI-influenced outcome — especially given automation of parts of the SDLC and production support.

(Safety & reliability is a close fourth given our production-critical systems, and is addressed within the Manage function below.)

3. Implementation Approach (NIST AI RMF: Govern · Map · Measure · Manage)

Principle 1 — Privacy & Security

NIST functionApproach at AT&T
GovernData-protection and AI-use policy; role-based access and consent rules; an AI governance owner and AI SPOCs accountable for data handling.
MapInventory which use cases process PII/CPNI, credit, and billing data; classify data sensitivity and legal basis per use case.
MeasureData-minimisation and de-identification checks; access-audit logs; security testing of AI pipelines; privacy-impact assessments.
ManageEnforce least-privilege access, encryption, and secure APIs; monitor for data leakage; incident-response runbook for AI data events.

Principle 2 — Transparency & Explainability

NIST functionApproach at AT&T
GovernStandard requiring documented model purpose, data lineage, and an explainability expectation for any customer- or revenue-impacting AI.
MapIdentify decisions where a human, regulator, or customer may need to understand the “why” (e.g., pricing, credit, order routing).
MeasureModel cards / documentation; explainability techniques for high-impact models; track the explainability-vs-accuracy trade-off explicitly.
ManageProvide reason codes and audit trails; disclose AI-assistance where appropriate; retire models that cannot be adequately explained for high-stakes use.

Principle 3 — Accountability

NIST functionApproach at AT&T
GovernNamed accountable owner per AI system; a model-risk review and approval gate before deployment; escalation and sign-off authority defined.
MapMap each AI output to the decision it influences and to the human who remains accountable for that decision.
MeasureTrack override rates, false-positive/negative rates, and review-coverage for customer-impacting outputs; periodic bias and performance audits.
ManageMandatory human-in-the-loop for high-impact decisions; change control on production-affecting models; a customer-redress and appeal path.

4. Governance Summary

These three principles are operationalised through one governance spine: a Responsible-AI policy (Govern), a per-use-case risk register that maps data, decisions, and owners (Map), a standard measurement set of privacy, explainability, bias, and reliability checks (Measure), and enforced controls with human-in-the-loop review and incident response (Manage). This makes Responsible AI a repeatable engineering discipline across the Sales Tooling AI roadmap rather than a case-by-case judgement.
